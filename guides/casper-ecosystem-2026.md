# The Casper ecosystem in 2026: exchanges, wallets, live and dead projects, Prove AI, grants and network incidents

Verified against casper-node v2.2.2 and docs.casper.network 2.0.0 on 2026-09-23.

This guide answers the questions about the Casper ecosystem that the official documentation does not: where CSPR trades today, which wallets, explorers and dApps still work and which are gone, what became of Casper Labs and what "Prove AI" is, who holds which public role, which grant and builder programmes exist and where their results are published, what happened in the July 2024 exploit and network pause, and every other mainnet halt or incident with a published account that this guide found. Every protocol fact is pinned to a casper-node release tag, a commit or a GitHub wiki revision. Almost everything else is a website, an exchange API or a live node response that was read on **2026-09-23** and can change the next day without a commit anywhere; each of those statements carries its date. Publication dates of casper.network news posts are the dates the site's own news index shows beside each post ([casper.network/news](https://www.casper.network/news) and its numbered pages, read 2026-09-23); most posts carry no date in their own text. A price, a prediction, and investment or tax advice are out of scope, and no figure here should be read as one.

## Who is who in 2026

- **Casper Association** describes itself as "a non-profit organization based in Switzerland" that "promotes the decentralized development and adoption of the Casper protocol, network and ecosystem" ([casper.network: Americorp Investments to bring US parking industry on-chain](https://www.casper.network/news/americorp-investments-us-parking-industry-on-chain-with-casper), posted 2025-05-14, read 2026-09-23). Since 2024 its own engineering team runs the core protocol: its 2024-09-17 release says the team "will now assume responsibility for the core protocol roadmap and upgrades" ([casper.network: new leadership, renewed focus](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-association-announces-new-leadership-renewed-focus-on-innovation-community), read 2026-09-23), and its October 2024 recap says the move "ends the previous outsourcing arrangement with Casper Labs" ([casper.network: X Space recap Oct 29, 2024](https://www.casper.network/news/x-space-recap-oct-29-2024), posted 2024-10-30, read 2026-09-23).
- **Casper Labs** is now **Prove AI AG**. A press release of 2024-10-22 says "Prove AI AG, formerly known as Casper Labs and initially built on the Casper Network, made the strategic decision to move to Hedera" ([GlobeNewswire, 2024-10-22](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/10/22/2967268/0/en/Prove-AI-Launches-on-the-Hedera-Network-Bringing-New-Standard-in-AI-Governance.html), read 2026-09-23), and on 2026-09-23 both `https://casperlabs.io/` and `https://www.casperlabs.io/` answered HTTP 301 with `Location: https://proveai.com/`. See *Casper Labs became Prove AI* below.
- **MAKE** builds the "CSPR Product Suite" — a casper.network post calls it "MAKE, the company behind the CSPR Product Suite" ([casper.network: Ghostminter](https://www.casper.network/news/what-you-can-build-with-ghostminter-a-new-token-launchpad), posted 2026-02-20, read 2026-09-23) — and it built Casper Wallet "with support from the Casper Association and Casper Labs" ([casper.network: Introducing Casper Wallet by MAKE](https://www.casper.network/news/introducing-casper-wallet-by-make), posted 2023-04-11, read 2026-09-23).

## Wallets: what works, and what is gone

- **Casper Wallet** (MAKE) is maintained: its newest GitHub release, [v2.8.0 of 2026-09-21](https://github.com/make-software/casper-wallet/releases/tag/v2.8.0), adds in-wallet token swap ("pick a pair, see the rate, price impact and route"), "CSPR and WCSPR wrap and unwrap through the same flow", and moves Ledger support onto "Ledger's Device Management Kit". It succeeded the Casper Signer ([casper.network](https://www.casper.network/news/introducing-casper-wallet-by-make), posted 2023-04-11, read 2026-09-23) and connects to desktop dApps from its mobile app over WalletConnect ([casper.network](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-wallet-integrates-walletconnect), posted 2025-10-16, read 2026-09-23). Its user guide and FAQ live on casperwallet.io, which asks automated readers not to fetch it; this guide does not quote them. The guide [Casper Wallet: setup, recovery, dApps, Ledger, swap and scam safety](casper-wallet.md) in this repository covers the extension from its v2.8.0 source.
- **Ledger**: Casper support in Ledger Live was announced by the app's developer, Zondax, on casper.network ([casper.network](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-network-now-supported-on-ledger-live-secure-your-assets-with-confidence), posted 2024-01-05, read 2026-09-23). The Ledger app's newest release is [Zondax/ledger-casper v3.1.8](https://github.com/Zondax/ledger-casper/releases/tag/v3.1.8), published 2026-05-26.
- **MetaMask, through the Casper Snap**: the docs list "Metamask with Casper Snap" ([build-on-casper.md#L21-L24](https://github.com/casper-network/docs-redux/blob/c78e3c2040d30fd8c42cb95bc8a3b308db9d1a56/versioned_docs/version-2.0.0/resources/build-on-casper.md?plain=1#L21-L24)). Its code is [casper-ecosystem/casper-manager](https://github.com/casper-ecosystem/casper-manager), last pushed 2025-05-18, and the npm package `casper-manager` has [2.0.0](https://www.npmjs.com/package/casper-manager) as its latest version, published 2025-02-20 (registry read 2026-09-23).
- **Social login through CSPR.click**: "a social login feature that allows users to log in using their Google or Apple account"; "When you sign in, a wallet is automatically created in the background" ([casper.network: CSPR.click Introduces Social Logins](https://www.casper.network/news/building-the-easiest-blockchain-ecosystem-to-access-cspr-click-introduces-social-logins), posted 2026-03-19, read 2026-09-23). The post says "The wallet is non-custodial, meaning only you can access the underlying cryptographic keys" and that users "can generate a recovery phrase, export your keys, or migrate to the Casper Wallet"; it does not say where the key is stored. On 2026-09-12 cspr.name offered `csprclick-w3a-google` and `csprclick-w3a-apple` beside `casper-wallet`, `ledger` and `metamask-snap` (see [the CSPR.name guide](cspr-name.md), *Deployed addresses the website published on 2026-09-12*).
- **Tangem** and **Burrito Wallet** (a Bithumb subsidiary's multi-chain wallet) were announced as supporting CSPR ([Tangem, posted 2024-11-25](https://www.casper.network/news/cspr-tangem-wallet); [Burrito, posted 2023-02-20](https://www.casper.network/news/introducing-the-bithumb-burrito-wallet); both read 2026-09-23). Whether they still do was not checked.
- **Gone: Casper Signer.** Its README says "Casper Signer is going to be deprecated soon, and new whitelisting requests are no longer accepted" and "The Casper Wallet replaces the Casper Signer" ([README.md at 16169472](https://github.com/casper-ecosystem/signer/blob/1616947294ec37a3fc721d6912b04b702770c6e2/README.md)); the repository's newest commit is dated 2023-07-27.
- **Gone: Casper Torus Wallet.** Web3Auth published a "Casper Torus Wallet Migration Guide" on 2024-07-24: "Casper Torus Wallet users are strongly encouraged to move their accounts to Casper Wallet", by logging in at `https://app.openlogin.com/` with the account "previously used for https://casper.tor.us/", downloading the private key there and choosing "Import Torus account" in Casper Wallet ([blog.web3auth.io](https://blog.web3auth.io/casper-wallet-migration-guide/), read 2026-09-23). Casper Wallet [v1.8.4](https://github.com/make-software/casper-wallet/releases/tag/v1.8.4) (2024-03-29) added the "Ability to import private keys from the Torus Wallet". On 2026-09-23 `casper.tor.us` did not exist in DNS and `app.openlogin.com` had no address record, so the site that guide names for the export step could not be reached that day.
- **Gone: CasperDash.** On 2026-09-23 `https://casperdash.io/` answered HTTP 522 (`error code: 522`, a Cloudflare origin timeout), the newest push to the wallet's own repositories in the [CasperDash GitHub organisation](https://github.com/CasperDash) was to `casperdash-extension` on 2024-03-03, and the newest push to any of the organisation's repositories was to `relay-contracts` on 2024-09-06 (GitHub API, read 2026-09-23).

### The 2021 outages

- **2021-11-03**: "Mainnet went down during the upgrade from 1.3.4 to 1.4.1": a race condition left most nodes without the final switch block, and a database rename in 1.4 stopped older versions from finding their database; the fix hard-linked the files back. "The network itself was unable to process new deploys from the start of the outage at block 283,521 (2021-11-03 13:50:46) until mainnet resumed normal operations at block 283,522 (2021-11-03 18:42:57) for a total outage of 4:21h." No blocks or confirmed deploys were lost ([wiki: Postmortem, revision 63e5caa6](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Postmortem/63e5caa61861ce4724796c9a4aecda96c622ad51)). The two block times it quotes are 4 h 52 min apart, and on 2026-09-23 a mainnet node returned the same times, block 283,521 at `2021-11-03T13:50:46.272Z` and block 283,522 at `2021-11-03T18:42:57.152Z`; the page's "4:21h" does not match them.
- **2021-11-22**: "high CPU usage in a cryptographic subroutine of the consensus process, triggered by an equivocation of a validator" led to a stall; the fix "deactivated the Limited Naiveté Criterion checks", rolled out as a patched 1.4.1 "over seven hours". The outage ran from block 332,816 to block 332,836, "a total outage of 9hrs 34mins", and "No blocks or transacted data was lost" (same page). A mainnet node returned block 332,816 timestamped `2021-11-22T17:40:52.864Z` and block 332,836 timestamped `2021-11-23T03:14:52.032Z` on 2026-09-23, matching.

### The 2023 upgrade outage

- **Before.** The operators' staging page set the 1.5.3 activation point at `Era 11081`, "approximately around" "2023-10-12 12:22 UTC", and described the release as "Security update and various bug fixes from 1.5.2" ([wiki: Upgrade to Casper node v1.5.3, revision 369b4d4c](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Upgrade-to-Casper-node-v1.5.3/369b4d4cf8e6c0dbb8fa49aa2a560c7dba619e18)).
- **During.** At 13:33 UTC on 2023-10-12 the Telegram channel Casper Official Announcements posted "We have identified an issue with the 1.5.3 mainnet upgrade. Our core team is working on a fix in coordination with the validators. Updates and a post-mortem will be shared soon." ([post 1203](https://t.me/casperofficialann/1203), read 2026-09-23).
- **The post-mortem**, posted in the casper-node repository's Discussions at 15:08 UTC the same day and not edited since ([discussion #4341](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/discussions/4341), read 2026-09-23): "Around 12:23 UTC mainnet went down due to a bug in the node software, during the upgrade from version 1.5.2 to 1.5.3." Migration code from the 1.5.2 upgrade ran again and changed nothing, "as planned", but "a final sanity check in the code ensuring that changes were made was erroneously firing, detecting no changes and shutting down the node", "resulting in a crash-restart loop for every node running 1.5.3". The fix was "a new, patched 1.5.3 binary with the unnecessary migration code disabled entirely", and "Official binaries have also been updated". It was not caught on testnet because the failing case "requires a validator to unbond across an upgrade point".
- **On chain.** On 2026-09-23 a mainnet node returned block 2,128,833 (protocol 1.5.3, era 11081, the first block of the new version) timestamped `2023-10-12T12:23:27.488Z` and the next block, 2,128,834, timestamped `2023-10-12T14:57:28.064Z`: a stop of 2 hours 34 minutes.

### The July 2024 exploit and network pause

The Association published a preliminary incident report and three updates; the report is the post-mortem of record.

- **What happened.** "On 26 July 2024, a security breach on a total of 13 accounts was detected." Root cause: "malicious actors exploited a vulnerability which allowed a contract installer to bypass the access rights check on urefs, thereby enabling them to grant the contract access to uref based resources. This escalation of privilege enabled illicit access including the ability to transfer tokens." The Association said "It is important to note that this was not related to the platform's architecture or consensus mechanisms" and put the "total amount of illicit transactions" at "around USD6.7 million" ([casper.network: Preliminary Incident Report](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-blockchain-security-update-preliminary-incident-report), posted 2024-07-31, read 2026-09-23).
- **Timeline, from the same report.** The team became aware at about 05:00 UTC on 26 July and identified the root cause at about 13:00 UTC; "a subset of validators agreed to pause consensus on 27 July 2024 - 07:50 UTC". An earlier update says "The Casper blockchain was halted by a decision of the validator community to contain the breach", and that by 28 July 17:30 UTC the Association was "confident that the extent of the security breach is limited to under 15 accounts" ([casper.network: Security Update on the July 26th Breach](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-blockchain-security-update-incident-contained-and-recovery-efforts-underway), posted 2024-07-29, read 2026-09-23).
- **Resumption.** "On 31 July 2024 at 15:18 UTC, 64 validators, representing 85 percent of CSPR staked on the Casper network, reached unanimous consent to resume validation" ([casper.network: Consensus Resumed](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-network-consensus-resumed-and-network-transactions-being-validated), posted 2024-08-01, read 2026-09-23); the post of 2024-08-02 says the same in other words ([casper.network: Incident Has Been Resolved](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-security-incident-has-been-resolved-network-is-fully-operational), read 2026-09-23).
- **Two blocks were replaced.** Because the pause came mid-era, "The decision was made to remove the two existing blocks back to the previous era's switch block". They held "1 mint of a CEP-78 NFT, and 3 native transfers totalling 2,023,040 CSPR", which were orphaned; the report lists their four deploy hashes and says "the two blocks removed from the chain at height 3,329,417 and height 3,329,418" would be "replaced with two new blocks with different hashes", with a `global_state.toml` applying "changes to global state" to "allow removal of illicit access from the attackers" (preliminary report, above). On 2026-09-23 a mainnet node returned block 3,329,416 (protocol 1.5.6, era 14543) and block 3,329,417 (protocol 1.5.7, era 14544, hash `0ac45ac8…37f`, not the `2e24b816…849` the report named) both timestamped `2024-07-27T07:49:27.168Z`, and block 3,329,418 timestamped `2024-07-31T15:18:59.328Z` — a gap of 103 hours 29 minutes.
- **The fix is public.** The node binary was an emergency release: the release note reads, in full, "Security Patch 1.5.7" ([release v1.5.7](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/releases/tag/v1.5.7)), and the operators' staging page for it reads "Patching of exploit and correct of global state data", with validators told to "force_run_version 1_5_7" once consensus was agreed ([wiki: Staging upgrade for 1.5.7, revision 5e088e21](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Staging-upgrade-for-1.5.7/5e088e2131745d2a43587fe7757ed5744e67f794)). The [41 commits from v1.5.6 to v1.5.7](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/compare/v1.5.6...v1.5.7) begin on 2024-07-26 with ["Add check for key validation during add contract version"](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/commit/446548ea282eedb7798c067a3d9027ead803ed2c), which makes `add_contract_version` call `validate_key` on every named key it is given ([runtime/mod.rs#L1722-L1724 at v1.5.7](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v1.5.7/execution_engine/src/core/runtime/mod.rs#L1722-L1724)), a regression test named `should_not_allow_forged_urefs_to_be_saved_to_named_keys`, marked `#[ignore]` at that tag ([regression_20240726.rs#L51-L53](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v1.5.7/execution_engine_testing/tests/src/test/regression/regression_20240726.rs#L51-L53)), and an ["Emergency update tool"](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/commit/26c3f86d01029ad6bd4e6ab22fcc77f731ca384e) in `global-state-update-gen`. The Association's report does not link these commits; the match between them and its root-cause description is this guide's reading.
- **Staking rewards and exchanges during the pause.** "During the time that the network was paused, there were no blocks proposed. Given the lack of block proposals, no rewards could be generated", and "Each exchange decides to halt trading or not independently" ([casper.network: AMA Recap Aug. 2nd 2024](https://www.casper.network/news/ama-recap-august-2nd-2024), posted 2024-08-05, read 2026-09-23).
- **Funds.** The report said "it is expected that substantial amounts will be recovered, with a further reimbursement procedure being put in place to cover any potential shortfall" (preliminary report, above). The last statement found is from October 2024: "The Association is working with law enforcement to recover blocked funds, though it is a lengthy process" ([recap posted 2024-10-30](https://www.casper.network/news/x-space-recap-oct-29-2024), read 2026-09-23).
- **A follow-up release** activated on 2024-08-09 at era 14653: casper-node 1.5.8, described as "an rapid update to include some regressions from Rust dependency libraries and auction configuration changes" ([wiki: Staging upgrade for 1.5.8, revision ff80fd37](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Staging-upgrade-for-1.5.8/ff80fd37d3b5961646eeb90b5d791dc6b09e2aff)). It carried the testnet `add_bid` remediation — "Increases the cost for add_bid to 3,500 CSPR" and "Introduces a 10,000 CSPR minimum bid" — for an attack where "At no time was Mainnet affected" (testnet notice, posted 2024-08-09, read 2026-09-23). Those two figures are history: the mainnet minimum bid at v2.2.2 is [500 CSPR](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L77).

### Security releases since, and what their notes say

Three later casper-node releases were marked as security releases, and their notes describe the change in one sentence at most: [v2.0.4](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/releases/tag/v2.0.4) ("Bug fix and security update."), [v2.2.1](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/releases/tag/v2.2.1) ("Transactions initiated by the System Account are never valid and are now blocked."; its staging page reads "Summary of changes in this upgrade: TBA (security upgrade)", [wiki revision 8fa62bfb](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Staging-for-MainNet-2.2.1-Upgrade/8fa62bfbe121a6adc43d983e3ee9d10646ecc8d8)) and [v2.2.2](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/releases/tag/v2.2.2) ("Security Release"; staging page likewise "TBA (security upgrade)", [wiki revision e6a426ca](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Staging-for-MainNet-2.2.2-Upgrade/e6a426caa2fd9c3ef6f92cc3f612c68876f8a01f)). None of them came with a halt, and none has a published post-mortem.

### Checking for halts since July 2024

No published account of a mainnet halt after August 2024 was found. On 2026-09-23 none of the 203 post titles in the casper.network news index names one; the Association's X Space and validator-call recaps read for this guide, from October 2024 to September 2026, mention none; none of the 29 pages of the casper-node wiki at its newest revision ([e6a426ca](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Staging-for-MainNet-2.2.2-Upgrade/e6a426caa2fd9c3ef6f92cc3f612c68876f8a01f), 2026-06-26) describes one; and no casper-node release note from v1.5.8 to v2.2.2 does. Because an absence of reports is weak evidence, the chain was checked as well, on 2026-09-23, through `chain_get_block` on `https://node.mainnet.casper.network/rpc`:

- Block timestamps were sampled every 20,000 blocks from block 3,329,419 (2024-07-31) to block 9,028,425 (2026-09-23). Every stretch whose average block time exceeded the median of its neighbours by more than ten minutes' worth, and the stretches around each upgrade, were then narrowed down to their largest single gap between consecutive blocks. No gap longer than 5.3 minutes was found. Stretches of 20,000 blocks naturally run up to about eight minutes shorter than their neighbours, so a pause could hide below the ten-minute mark only if it was shorter than about twenty minutes; no pause longer than that can have gone unnoticed in that range.
- The largest gaps found are the restarts at the seven protocol upgrades in that range, each measured from the first block of the new version to the next block: 2.5 min after block 3,376,274 (1.5.8, 2024-08-09), 2.1 min after block 4,793,043 (2.0.0, 2025-05-06), 5.3 min after block 5,573,015 (2.0.4, 2025-10-01, `14:46:49` to `14:52:07` UTC), 1.1 min after block 5,947,071 (2.1.1, 2025-12-11), 2.6 min after block 7,046,783 (2.2.0, 2026-03-23), 3.2 min after block 7,660,304 (2.2.1, 2026-05-19) and 2.6 min after block 8,112,792 (2.2.2, 2026-06-30). The operators' staging page had set the 2.0.4 upgrade for "Era 19665, around 2025-10-01 14:46 UTC" ([wiki: Staging for MainNet 2.0.4, revision 285ca5c8 of 2025-09-29](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/wiki/Staging-for-MainNet-2.0.4-Upgrade/285ca5c84bfad0b477e9324f8e8e1b7c56ee7efd)).
- The largest gap found outside an upgrade restart, 4.2 min, followed block 5,573,135 on 2025-10-01 (`15:43:11` to `15:47:24` UTC). It fell within about two hours of slower blocks after the 2.0.4 upgrade: blocks 5,573,016 to 5,573,319 (14:52 to 16:50 UTC) averaged 23.4 s each, against 16.4 s for the 300 blocks before the upgrade and the 300 after that stretch.
- For scale: mainnet's `minimum_block_time` is 8,000 ms at v2.2.2 ([chainspec#L32](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L32)); the sample shows about 16.5 s per block before the 2.1.1 upgrade and about 8.0 s after it.

So a report of a "network halt" after July 2024 does not match the chain: the only pauses in block production found since then are the restarts of one to about five minutes at protocol upgrades listed above, and one 4.2-minute gap during the slow period after the 2.0.4 upgrade. Ask which date the person means before answering.

## Corrections: published pages that are out of date

As of 2026-09-23:

1. **The documentation's tool list.** "Casper.info", "Div3.in" and "casperholders.io" are listed as working tools ([build-on-casper.md#L26-L33](https://github.com/casper-network/docs-redux/blob/c78e3c2040d30fd8c42cb95bc8a3b308db9d1a56/versioned_docs/version-2.0.0/resources/build-on-casper.md?plain=1#L26-L33)); none of them is (see *Explorers*). Use CSPR.live.
2. **The csprUSD post's fee claim.** It says Casper "addressed this challenge with the adoption of CEP-97, reducing the minimum transaction cost from 2.5 CSPR to just 0.1 CSPR" ([casper.network](https://www.casper.network/news/introducing-csprusd-the-stablecoin-of-the-machine-economy), posted 2026-07-29, read 2026-09-23). What mainnet does instead, on 2026-09-23:
   - [CEP-97](https://github.com/casper-network/ceps/blob/3748c9017218378e650d8e5731feaf82088657bd/text/0097-remove-custom-payment.md) is a proposal, and no casper-node release implements it; v2.2.2 is the newest release.
   - A native transfer already cost 0.1 CSPR before the post: the mint's `transfer` cost is `100_000_000` motes ([chainspec#L473](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L473)) and the mint lane's gas limit is `100_000_000` ([chainspec#L224](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L224)). For example, `info_get_transaction` for native-transfer deploy `c81fdf6114a03b0b265ff2baa2895fc1d9a8d7f0aaff00833a2e798112bb85f8` (block 9,028,911) reported a limit and a cost of 100,000,000 motes.
   - A transaction that runs contract code, such as a token transfer, must still pay at least `baseline_motes_amount`, 2,500,000,000 motes (2.5 CSPR), which the chainspec calls "the penalty payment amount, the lowest cost, and the minimum balance amount" ([chainspec#L178-L179](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L178-L179)); a smaller payment is rejected as `InvalidPaymentAmount` ([deploy.rs#L570-L572](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/types/src/transaction/deploy.rs#L570-L572), [meta_transaction_v1.rs#L568-L570](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/node/src/types/transaction/meta_transaction/meta_transaction_v1.rs#L568-L570)); mainnet refunds 75% of the unused part (`refund_ratio = [75, 100]`, [chainspec#L117](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L117)). For example, `info_get_transaction` for `26e497386fdad411598337ff90ab210d3bfb292857a750595646af8bfbf1bd07` (block 9,028,923, a call to a contract's `approve` entry point) reported a limit and a cost of 2,500,000,000 motes, 417,375,978 motes consumed and a refund of 1,561,968,016 motes.
   - `native_transfer_minimum_motes`, also 2.5 CSPR, is "The minimum amount in motes for a valid native transfer" ([chainspec#L199-L200](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L199-L200)): the smallest amount a native transfer may move, not a fee.
   - The live mainnet chainspec returned by `info_get_chainspec` on 2026-09-23 had the same values. The guide [Casper roadmap and EVM status](casper-roadmap-and-evm.md), section *CEP-97: accepted as a proposal, not implemented*, covers the proposal in full.
3. **A September 2024 launch of Prove AI on Casper** was announced in two Association posts: "Prove AI, an AI governance solution being jointly developed by Casper Labs and IBM that will launch on the Casper network in September" ([posted 2024-08-02](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-security-incident-has-been-resolved-network-is-fully-operational)) and "Prove AI will launch on the Casper network in September" ([AMA recap posted 2024-08-05](https://www.casper.network/news/ama-recap-august-2nd-2024)); both read 2026-09-23. Prove AI moved to Hedera on 2024-10-22 instead.
4. **The Casper Accelerate page** still says "Apply Now" and that applications are reviewed "on a rolling basis", but its URL is under `/suspended/` and its footer link is hidden (read 2026-09-23). Do not present it as open unless the Association announces that it is.
5. **"A post-mortem will be made available after resolution"** ([posted 2024-07-29](https://www.casper.network/news/casper-blockchain-security-update-incident-contained-and-recovery-efforts-underway), read 2026-09-23): the only report in the news index is the one titled "Preliminary Incident Report" (posted 2024-07-31).
6. **The testnet notice's "10,000 CSPR minimum bid"** (posted 2024-08-09) is not the mainnet rule today; mainnet's is 500 CSPR at v2.2.2 ([chainspec#L77](https://github.com/casper-network/casper-node/blob/v2.2.2/resources/mainnet/chainspec.toml#L77)).

## What this guide does not cover, and what nobody publishes

This guide does not cover prices, market capitalisation, trading volume, token forecasts, or investment or tax questions, and none of its figures should be used to answer one. It does not explain how to use any exchange, wallet or dApp; their own documentation does. As of 2026-09-23, the following could not be verified from any published source, and nothing below should be answered from memory:

- **A final post-mortem of the July 2024 exploit.** Only the preliminary report was found, and no outcome of the fund recovery or the reimbursement procedure has been published. Nothing identifying the attackers has been published either.
- **The "Incident Analysis report"** that the Association said in January 2024 it expected "to share … in the next few weeks", and the "comprehensive report" promised after the Discord breach. Neither appears in the news index.
- **What the security releases fixed.** v2.0.4, v2.2.1 and v2.2.2 have one-line notes; no advisory has been published for any of them.
- **A mainnet status page or incident feed.** None was found; the halt check above was done by reading block timestamps.
- **Forum posts about incidents.** The Casper forum was not searched in bulk, which its policy allows only for targeted fetches of specific topics; a web search for incident post-mortems there found none.
- **The Agentic Buildathon's prize amounts per team and any track awards.** The placings are published on forward.casper.network (see *Grants*); anything more is on DoraHacks, which could not be read. The Casper Hackathon 2026 liquid-staking-track winner was not named in any post found.
- **A current grant programme**, or an announcement that Casper Accelerate is suspended or closed.
- **Current roles of Mrinal Manohar and Medha Parlikar**: nothing was found after 2025-06-18 for him or after 2024-05-28 for her (see the People table).
- **One authoritative exchange list.** casper.network's list and the exchanges' own APIs disagree (Bitstamp still names CSPR but has no CSPR market, and is not on casper.network's list). Venues this guide did not query are neither confirmed nor ruled out, and which countries may use a market is not published in any source read here.
- **The Casper Wallet user guide** on casperwallet.io, which asks automated readers not to fetch it.
- **Where a CSPR.click social-login wallet's key is stored.** The announcement says "The wallet is non-custodial, meaning only you can access the underlying cryptographic keys" and that users "can generate a recovery phrase, export your keys, or migrate to the Casper Wallet"; it does not say where the key is kept.
- **Whether a Casper Torus Wallet key can still be exported**, now that the site Web3Auth's migration guide names for the export has no address record (2026-09-23). No newer instructions were found in the sources read here.
- **A mainnet contract for the updated csprUSD**, and whether Friendly Market's DEX, Tangem or Burrito Wallet are in use on Casper 2.x.
- **Casper TVL.** No tracker read here publishes one; DefiLlama lists no Casper protocol.
